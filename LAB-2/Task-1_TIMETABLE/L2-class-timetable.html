Abstract— This electronic document is a “live” template and already defines the components of your paper, including title, text, headings, and other elements. This template provides the formatting specifications needed for preparing electronic versions of conference papers.
Keywords— component, formatting, style, styling, insert, keywords

I. INTRODUCTION

This template provides authors with most of the formatting specifications needed for preparing electronic versions of their papers. All standard paper components have been specified for ease of use when formatting individual papers, automatic compliance with electronic requirements, and conformity of style throughout conference proceedings.

Margins, column widths, line spacing, and type styles are built into the template. Examples of the type styles are provided throughout the document.

II. EASE OF USE

A. Selecting a Template

First, confirm that you have the correct template for your paper size. This template has been tailored for output on US-letter paper size. If you are using A4-sized paper, use the appropriate A4 format.

B. Maintaining the Integrity of the Specifications

The template is used to format the paper and style the text. All margins, column widths, line spaces, and text fonts are prescribed. These specifications should not be changed.

III. PREPARE YOUR PAPER BEFORE STYLING

Before formatting the paper, first write and save the content as a separate text file. Complete all content and organizational editing before formatting.

Keep text and graphic files separate until after the text has been formatted and styled. Do not use hard tabs and limit the use of hard returns to one return at the end of a paragraph.

A. Abbreviations and Acronyms

Define abbreviations and acronyms the first time they are used, even after they have been defined in the abstract. Common abbreviations such as IEEE, SI, MKS, CGS, sc, dc, and rms do not have to be defined.

B. Units

Use either SI or CGS units as primary units. English units may be used as secondary units in parentheses when needed.

  • Use SI units whenever possible.
  • Avoid combining SI and CGS units.
  • Do not mix complete spellings and abbreviations.
  • Use a zero before decimal points.

C. Equations

Equations are an exception to some of the prescribed specifications. Equations should be numbered consecutively and centered within the column.

a + b = γ      (1)

Symbols used in equations should be defined before or immediately following the equation. Equation numbers should be placed on the right side.

D. Some Common Mistakes

  • The word “data” is plural, not singular.
  • Do not confuse the subscript for scientific constants.
  • Pay attention to punctuation when using quotation marks.
  • Do not confuse “affect” and “effect”.
  • Do not confuse “imply” and “infer”.

IV. USING THE TEMPLATE

After the text edit has been completed, the paper is ready for the template. Duplicate the template and use the naming convention prescribed by the conference.

A. Authors and Affiliations

The template is designed for, but not limited to, six authors. A minimum of one author is required for conference articles. Author names should be listed from left to right and then move down to the next line.

B. Identify the Headings

Headings organize the topics in a paper. There are component heads and text heads. Text heads organize topics in a relational and hierarchical manner.

C. Figures and Tables

Figures and tables should generally be placed at the top or bottom of columns. Large figures and tables may span both columns.
